<h2 class="section-title">Course Description</h2>
<p>Computing innovations bring an era of exciting new technologies to society like social networks, microblogging, the Internet of Things, autonomous vehicles, and chatbots. At the same time, society is growing increasingly concerned that these new capabilities bring a host of unwelcome side effects. Recent concerns include election interference, surveillance capitalism, and killer robots. How can we critically assess whether a technology causes more harm than good? This course covers the ethical, societal, and policy implications of new computing technology. It situates recent developments in computing and their impact on society in the context of fundamental principles from computing, policy, ethics, and social sciences. The first part of the class covers foundational issues such as privacy, fairness, personal autonomy, agency, and sustainability. The remainder of the course focuses on challenges presented by recent technological developments and analyses of these new situations as they emerge at the interface of computing and society.</p>
